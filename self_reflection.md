Self-Reflection on Learning in UCUG1600 Cross-Cultural Communication

When I signed up for UCUG1600, I thought cross-cultural communication was mostly about knowing the right things to say around people from different backgrounds. That assumption didn't survive the first month. The course kept pushing me to rethink things I didn't even know I assumed, and by the end I was making arguments I never expected to make. A field trip to Macau changed how I read urban space altogether.

选这门课的时候，我以为跨文化交流就是"知道在来自不同文化背景的人面前该说什么"。这个想法在第一个月就被打碎了。课程不断推着我重新审视那些我甚至不知道自己持有的假设，到最后我提出了一些从未想过会提出的论点。一次澳门实地考察彻底改变了我阅读城市空间的方式。

---

In our final group project, I ended up taking the lead on the report. Our topic, "Curating Macau: How One Route Stages Intercultural Identity," was not handed to us. It emerged from a pile of raw field trip footage that I downloaded and sifted through, pulling out images from six or seven locations, trying to figure out what story these places were telling together. I wrote the first draft of the presentation structure and the report's main argument. When teammates pushed back, saying my literature review section felt too dense, I restructured rather than gutted it. Proposing, getting challenged, refining. That cycle taught me more about collaborative work than I expected.

在最终小组项目中，我承担了报告的主要撰写工作。我们的主题"策展中的澳门：一条路线如何编排跨文化身份"不是被分配的。它是我从一堆实地拍摄的原始素材中提炼出来的。我下载了六七个地点的视频，逐帧提取图片，试图弄清楚这些地方共同讲述着什么故事。我写了展示结构和报告核心论点的初稿。当队友反馈说文献综述部分太重时，我选择了重组而非删减。提出、被质疑、再调整。这个循环教会我的协作经验，比我想象的多。

---

The classroom discussions had a way of forcing me to put words to ideas I had only vaguely felt before. Take the tutorial on Disney's Mulan and cultural narrative reshaping. I found myself arguing, somewhat to my own surprise, that Mulan's return to domestic life after the war was not a failure of her character arc. She had adapted strategically to the conditions of her time: in a chaotic era, competence mattered more than gender, and she exploited that opening. In peacetime, those openings narrowed. The bigger issue was how Disney reshaped the story. They needed a happy ending. Family-film warmth. A strong female character who "has it all." None of this comes from the original Chinese material. Disney dressed up a cultural rewrite as celebration.

课堂讨论有一种力量，逼我把那些模糊感受到的东西说出来。比如讨论迪士尼《花木兰》与文化叙事重塑的那次 tutorial。我发现自己提出了一个连自己都有些意外的论点：花木兰战后回归家庭并不是她角色弧的失败。她是在策略性地适应那个时代的条件。乱世之中，能力比性别更重要，她利用了那个缝隙。而到了和平年代，这些缝隙就收窄了。但更大的问题是迪士尼如何重塑了整个故事。他们需要一个圆满结局。家庭电影的温暖基调。一个"事业家庭双丰收"的强大女性角色。这些东西没有一样来自中国原始素材。迪士尼把一次文化改写包装成了赞颂。

---

The guest lecture on sports and national identity left me thinking for days. Professor Lv walked us through several layers of what "representing a country" means in the context of Chinese sports: naturalized athletes like Li Kai'er who look Chinese and strongly identify as Chinese; athletes who do not look Chinese but have fully adopted the identity; and figures like Eileen Gu who carry dual nationality and the contradictions that come with it. I had never thought about identity as something with layers before. I had always treated it as a binary, either-or question. That lecture made me realize how much of the "national identity debate" in sports is actually about something else entirely: anxieties about who belongs where, about who gets to decide what "Chinese" means.

关于体育与国家身份的客座讲座让我想了很久。吕教授带着我们逐层分析了在体育语境下"代表一个国家"意味着什么：像李凯尔这样长得像中国人、也强烈认同中国身份的规划运动员；外表不像中国人但已经完全接纳了中国身份的运动员；还有像谷爱凌这样承载双重国籍及其矛盾的人物。我以前从来没有把"身份"理解为一个分层的东西。我总是把它当作非此即彼的二元问题。那次讲座让我意识到，体育中所谓的"国家身份之争"其实是关于完全不同的东西：关于谁属于哪里，关于谁来定义什么是中国人。

---

A similar shift happened when we discussed the BBC documentary Chinese School. The surface-level reading was obvious: Chinese teaching methods clashed with British students. But the more we talked, the clearer it became that this was not about teaching styles at all. The real gap was underneath: how each society understands the relationship between individual and collective, and what authority means in a classroom. Calling it a "method difference" misses the point. It is a difference in what education is for.

类似的转变发生在讨论 BBC 纪录片《中式学校》的时候。表面解读很显然：中国式教学方法跟英国学生产生了冲突。但随着讨论的深入，我越来越清楚这根本不是教学方式的问题。真正的差距在底层：每个社会如何理解个人与集体的关系，课堂中的权威意味着什么。把它称为"方法差异"完全没抓住重点。这是"教育到底为了什么"的差异。

---

One of the earliest tutorial activities that stuck with me was the non-verbal communication exercise. We were given words and had to express them to a partner using only body language. I expected it to be awkward and limited. It worked. That experience connected to Edward Hall's idea of space as a "silent language" and the concept of high-context communication, where meaning travels through gestures and situational cues rather than precise grammar. I had always assumed that cross-cultural communication was primarily a language problem. But if you have the right semantic elements, "me," "water," and a drinking gesture, the grammar barely matters. Your brain fills in the rest. Communication just works that way.

最早让我留下深刻印象的 tutorial 活动之一是非语言交流练习。我们被给定词汇，只能用肢体语言向搭档表达。我本以为会很尴尬、很局限。结果管用。这次体验直接呼应了 Edward Hall 关于"空间作为无声语言"的理论和高语境沟通的概念，意义通过手势和情境线索传递，而非精确的语法。我一直以为跨文化交流主要是一个语言问题。但如果你有正确的语义要素，"我"、"水"、加一个喝水的动作，语法几乎不重要。你的大脑会自动补全剩下的部分。沟通就是这么运作的。

---

The Macau field trip brought all of these threads together. Before the trip, "East-meets-West fusion" felt like a perfectly adequate description of Macau. After walking through the city, that label collapsed. Our project identified five distinct spatial logics along a single route: the border as institutional threshold, commercial streets as tourist consumption, the historic district as embodied heritage reading, Cotai's entertainment complexes as global spectacle, and transit spaces as everyday cultural overlap.

澳门实地考察把这些线索全部串联了起来。在出发之前，"中西融合"似乎是对澳门非常充分的描述。走完这座城市后，这个标签崩塌了。我们的项目识别出沿着一条路线运作的五种截然不同的空间逻辑：口岸的制度化边界、商业街的旅游消费、历史城区的身体化遗产阅读、路氹娱乐综合体的全球奇观、以及交通空间中日常文化的重叠。

---

On a Macau public bus, I sat surrounded by Catholic nuns, local residents heading home, construction workers, and tourists. Nobody was performing cultural exchange.

在一辆澳门公交车上，我周围是天主教修女、回家的本地居民、建筑工人和游客。没有人刻意进行什么文化交流。

It was just a bus.

它只是一辆公交车。

But that ordinariness was exactly the point. Hall writes about spatial arrangements as a form of non-verbal communication, and here it was: a space where different cultures simply existed alongside each other in daily rhythm. That bus ride reinforced something I had been circling around all semester. The cross-cultural experiences that stay with you are not always the ones designed to teach you something.

但这种平凡恰恰就是重点。Hall 写过空间秩序本身就是一种非语言交流，而此刻它就在眼前：一个不同文化在日常生活节奏中简单共处的空间。那次公交之旅强化了我整个学期一直在靠近的一个认识。留在你心里的跨文化体验，不总是那些被设计来教育你的。

---

Looking back, the most important shift in my thinking was learning to resist the urge to label before understanding. Before this course, I would have looked at the Venetian in Macau and called it "fake Europe." Our analysis pushed me to ask instead: how does it actually function? Who is it for? What kind of authenticity does it produce through design and participation? The same impulse carried into the Mulan discussion; instead of judging her ending through a Western lens, I learned to situate it in its own context. And in the Chinese School debate, I stopped asking which system was better and started asking why each system makes sense within its own cultural framework. Moving from labeling to analyzing, from "what is it?" to "how does it work, and for whom?" That shift is what I value most from this course.

回头看，我思维中最重要的转变是学会了在理解之前抵制贴标签的冲动。修这门课之前，看到澳门的威尼斯人，我会直接叫它"假欧洲"。而我们的分析迫使我换一种问法：它实际上如何运作？为谁服务？它通过设计和参与生产了什么样的真实性？同样的思维惯性也延伸到了花木兰的讨论，我不再用西方视角评判她的结局，而是学会了将她放回自身的语境中。在中式学校的辩论中，我不再追问哪种制度更好，而是开始追问为什么每种制度在自身的文化框架内是合理的。从贴标签到分析，从"它是什么"到"它如何运作，为谁服务"。这个转变是这门课给我最有价值的东西。

---

If I could offer suggestions for the course, a few things come to mind. The field trip component is one of the strongest parts of the experience, but expanding destination options beyond locations near campus would expose students to a wider range of cross-cultural environments. I also found that sessions built around concrete cases generated far more engagement than abstract conceptual lectures. Whether it was sports and identity or the Chinese School documentary, having something specific to argue about made the material feel closer. Group projects would also benefit from being broken into weekly milestones with specific subtasks. Without that structure, it is too easy for a team to drift until the deadline forces a rush, and the quality of collaboration suffers.

如果让我对课程提建议，有几件事想说。Field trip 是这门课最有价值的部分之一，但如果能提供更多目的地选择，不仅限于学校附近，学生会接触到更丰富的跨文化环境。我也发现围绕具体案例展开的课堂比抽象的概念讲授激发了更多参与感。不管是体育与身份还是中式学校纪录片，有具体的东西可以争论，课程内容就感觉更贴近。小组项目如果能拆分为按周设定的子任务节点，协作质量也会好很多。没有这种结构，团队很容易一路松散到 deadline 前才匆忙赶工，协作质量跟着受损。

---

This course did not teach me what to think about other cultures. It taught me how to look more carefully and with less certainty, to sit with complexity rather than resolve it into a tidy label. That is a harder skill than I expected. I suspect I will keep learning it long after the semester ends.

这门课没有教我"怎么看待其他文化"。它教我怎么去看：更仔细地看，带着更少的确信，带着与复杂性共处的意愿，而不是急于把它化解成一个漂亮的标签。这比我预想的要难。我猜在学期结束很久以后，我还会继续学。
